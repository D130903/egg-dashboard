Key insight

Supermarkets sold about the same number of eggs both years (around 248 million dozen), but shoppers spent more: $1.37bn rose to $1.58bn. Higher prices, not more eggs sold, caused the growth.

Organic is not listed separately. It is counted inside Specialty with other niche eggs, so organic is at most the Specialty figure. Pasteurised eggs (heat-treated for safety) are mostly sold as liquid egg to restaurants and food makers, not in supermarket cartons. No share figure is published. In 2024-25 Australia imported 1,712 tonnes of liquid egg ($8.8m) and exported 389 tonnes ($2.2m).

Case study: Josh's Rainbow Eggs (free range, Victoria)

A small farm that grew by selling free-range eggs through the big supermarkets (news.com.au report; figures as reported then)
Number of hens

Start 40 hens left on the farm; a 9-year-old began selling the eggs to neighbours.

2012 First worker hired, 1,500 hens.

2015 10,000 hens and 8 full-time staff. After an email to Coles, the family met head office and won shelf space, then Woolworths followed.

Later Over $5m a year in sales, stocked in more than 100 supermarkets.

What it shows: Free range is the biggest egg type (56.6% of eggs sold) and shoppers pay about $6.87 a dozen for it. Winning supermarket shelf space was the growth step for this producer. Supermarket own brands now make up 54.2% of eggs sold, so named brands like this one compete for the rest.

Timeline of supply shocks

2022-23 National shortage from volatile demand, rising free-range share and poor weather.

2024 Three strains of H7 avian influenza in Victoria, NSW and ACT: the largest flock loss on record, about 7% of capacity.

Early 2025 Further Victorian outbreak (about 1.8m birds culled in total); supermarkets cap carton purchases.

2024-25 Hens are replaced (+17%) but fewer eggs are produced (573m dozen) because new hens take 18-20 weeks to start laying.

2025 Major supermarkets complete caged-egg phase-out; cage egg sales fall about 32%.

Risk H5N1 confirmed in wild birds in parts of Australia, a threat to layer farms.

Sources: Australian Eggs Annual Reports 2024 and 2025 (production, flock, state shares, per capita, supermarket sales data from Circana); Dept of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (value of eggs at the farm); news.com.au (Josh's Rainbow Eggs case study); ABS; ABARES via NSW DPI and IBISWorld; ANZ Research. Typical price per dozen is calculated, not reported: money spent divided by dozens sold. For each type, it is its share of money divided by its share of eggs, times the $6.36 average. It covers big supermarkets only. Eggs eaten per person was reported at 267 after the 2025 report came out.
